# Voice

Source of truth for site copy. `CLAUDE.md` requires first person, plain, specific writing that leads with the problem and the number.

## Rules

- First person, plain, specific. Lead with the problem and the number.
- Avoid: "passionate" / "passionately", "leveraging", "cutting-edge", "synergy".
- No claim without a measurement or a named source.

## Samples

### Sample 1 — leadership essay

> The techniques and practices that make a good leader are often viewed in a unidimensional manner as if there is only a single truth that defines how a good leader must guide their employees. Directing and encouraging employees to ensure their assigned tasks are completed efficiently doesn't always entail them eventually developing the mindset to automatically discern areas of improvements and fixtures, rather than expecting their leadership to present them with the problems. Organizations usually expect their employees to develop a business driven mindset looking beyond conventional problem solving and develop a proactive mindset towards continuous modernizations. Hence leaders are often faced with considering a directive as well as an empowering mindset when guiding their employees, discerning what style of leadership suits them best depending on the needs and situations of each employee

### Sample 2 — cover letter

> What particularly excites me about this role is the opportunity to work on ABC's distributed, fault-tolerant database system. Hearing firsthand from the team on their approach to creating seamless, real-time data flow across edge and cloud platforms represents a sophisticated engineering challenge that I'm deeply passionate about solving. Furthermore, I believe my proficiency in developing applications across multiple software platforms would help me in tackling the complex challenges of building fault-tolerant and scalable systems.
> Beyond technical skills, I bring a collaborative mindset and passion for continuous learning that I discerned to be central to ABC's culture. My experience working across diverse teams and technologies, positions me as an adaptable and flexible professional who thrives in dynamic, fast-paced environments.

### What these samples establish

- The natural register is formal and essayistic: long, layered sentences with
  subordinate clauses ("Directing and encouraging employees to ensure their
  assigned tasks are completed efficiently doesn't always entail...").
- Abstract framing comes first; concrete detail comes later, if at all.
- "Discern", "entail", "hence" and "furthermore" appear naturally and can stay.
- Sample 2 contains "passionate" and "dynamic, fast-paced environments", which
  the rules above ban. The structure can be kept; those phrases should not.
- Neither sample contains a number, a named system, or a specific incident, so
  they do not by themselves supply the "problem and the number" the site needs.

### How to write site copy from these

Keep the sentence architecture — one main clause, then the qualifying detail —
but add the missing specificity: name the system, say what broke, give the
measured result. Do not imitate the cover-letter vocabulary.
